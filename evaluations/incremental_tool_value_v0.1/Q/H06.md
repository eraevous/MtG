### 1. Fails: Necroskitter + your own Puppeteer Clique (persist). The deciding condition is the controller.

On keywords this looks like a perfect fit: persist returns Clique with a -1/-1 counter, and Necroskitter cares about creatures that die with a -1/-1 counter. Both are in Blight Curse [tool: inventory/decision_grade]. But Necroskitter's Oracle text reads "Whenever a creature **an opponent controls** with a -1/-1 counter on it dies" [tool: rules_text/decision_grade]. When your persisted Clique dies a second time, it has the counter, but you control it. Necroskitter never triggers, and persist doesn't apply either, because the counter is already there [model rules knowledge]. The creature that Clique reanimates is a second case of the same failure. It is "put … onto the battlefield under your control" [tool: rules_text/decision_grade], and the Clique exiles it at your next end step. While it's yours, Necroskitter ignores it, and the exile doesn't count as dying anyway [model rules knowledge].

### 2. Comparable pair that works: Necroskitter + Black Sun's Zenith, against opposing creatures.

Black Sun's Zenith reads "Put X -1/-1 counters on each creature" [tool: rules_text/decision_grade]. Opposing creatures whose toughness drops to 0 die from state-based actions with those counters still on them. They are controlled by an opponent and carry a -1/-1 counter, which satisfies every condition on Necroskitter, so you may return each of them under your control [model rules knowledge]. Two rules details back this up [model rules knowledge]:
- "Dies" triggers look back in time (CR 603.10a), so the game checks the counter and the controller on the creature as it last existed.
- That look-back also means Necroskitter still triggers if X is 4 or more and it dies at the same moment. I haven't confirmed its 1/4 body with the tool; it's from memory.

The returned cards come back as new objects with no counters (CR 400.7). The difference from pair 1 is controller alone: same card, same counter type, same death event.
